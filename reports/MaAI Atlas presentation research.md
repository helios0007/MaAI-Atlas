# Grow a map where the blog fails

MaAI Atlas has a strong, citable case on all four fronts. The IAAC blog holds **4,227 student posts written by 758 different authors**. Students publish them themselves, and no public template tells them how. Visitors can browse the blog only by programme, year, term and course. They cannot search by method, tool or dataset. **27.6% of posts have no tags**, and **only 2% of posts link to GitHub**. The slime-mould story gives a tested method for drawing the links that the blog does not show. A brainless mould rebuilt the Tokyo rail network in 26 hours. Jones turned that behaviour into simple agent rules. Astronomers then used those rules to trace the cosmic web through about 37,000 galaxies, and Hubble data confirmed the result. The Sagrada Família gives an exact metaphor as of November 2026. All six central towers are complete on the outside, and the Tower of Jesus Christ was blessed on 10 June 2026. But the Glory façade, the main entrance, is not built, and the forecast end of works is now about 2035 to 2036. For the slides, the assertion-evidence method fits your "headings and visuals only" plan: each heading is a one-sentence claim, and the explanation goes in a script of about 130 to 150 words per minute. Two cautions apply to the whole talk. First, no public source shows students complaining about the blog, so claim only the problems you measured. Second, the Gaudí quote "my client is not in a hurry" has no primary source, so present it as "attributed".

## 4,227 self-published posts and no public rulebook

The blog at blog.iaac.net runs on WordPress. It holds **4,227 posts and 104,407 media files**, dated from 19 October 2022 to 25 November 2026 ([Posts API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?per_page=1); [Media API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/media?per_page=1)). Students publish their own work. Each student registers through a public form that asks for a programme (15 options, including "Master in AI for Architecture and the Built Environment 01/02") and an academic year ([Register page](https://blog.iaac.net/register/)). The blog has **778 user accounts**, and their slugs are personal names ([Users API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/users?per_page=100)). Authorship is very spread out: **758 different primary authors** wrote the 4,227 posts, and the most active account has only 19 posts. The Co-Authors Plus plugin is active, and **58% of posts (2,444) have two or more co-authors**, which matches group work ([REST root](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/); [Posts API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?_fields=id,coauthors)).

The instructions for publishing exist only inside courses. An MAA 24/25 Advanced Computational Design final brief tells students to post a blog entry on the course sub-blog at blog.iaac.net. It lists the Grasshopper scripts as a separate deliverable and says "further instructions will be provided before the final deadline". The MaAI 25/26 syllabi ask for "your selection of a few images to be published on IAAC blog", and a separate Google Form collects MaAI final submissions (IAAC course documents, 2025-26). This is a key point for the talk. The official route splits one project into three places: the blog post, the code, and the form. Web searches found no public IAAC guide, style sheet or checklist for blog posts. So each student decides the structure, the tags and the links.

MaAI is small and new on the blog. It has **77 posts, and the first one is from 6 December 2024**. MAA has 1,788 posts, MaCAD 825 and MaCT 574 ([Categories API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/categories?per_page=100); [Posts API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/posts)). This is a good argument for the atlas: the MaAI corpus is small enough to map in full now, and it will only grow.

The blog navigation follows the school's administration: programme, cohort, year, term, course. It does not follow the questions a visitor asks. Programme pages filter by course year, term and course, and the search page has one keyword box with no facets ([MaAI programme page](https://blog.iaac.net/projects/maai-master-in-ai-for-architecture-and-the-built-environment/); [Search page](https://blog.iaac.net/search/)). The course taxonomy has **590 terms; 97 have zero posts and 208 have three or fewer**. Its labels are not consistent: "MaAI 24/25", "MaAI01 25/26", "MAAI01 25/26" and "MRAC01 MAAI 25/26" all appear. One term has the slug `maai01-24-25-ai-theory` but the label "MaAI 25/26 AI Theory" ([Course API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/course?per_page=100)). The course filter also misses real work. The MaAI 24/25 "AI for Robotic Fabrication Workshop" term has zero posts, but a MaAI post about that workshop exists ([example post](https://blog.iaac.net/ai-for-robotic-fabrication-workshop-reinforcement-learning-for-intelligent-grid-based-carving/)).

Tags cannot fill the gap. There are only **58 tags**. They mix domains, tools, materials and course names, and they have no tag for most AI methods: there is no "machine-learning", "computer-vision", "reinforcement-learning" or "llm" tag ([Tags API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/tags?per_page=100&orderby=count)). **1,166 posts (27.6%) have no tags, including 13 of the 77 MaAI posts** ([Posts API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?_fields=id,tags)). The result is simple to say on a slide: a visitor can find "MaAI 24/25 Research Studio", but cannot find "all projects that use reinforcement learning" or "all projects with code" without reading posts one by one.

Code and data have no field in the post. Links are only free text in the body. Across all posts, **85 link to GitHub (2.0%)**; for MaAI the figure is 8 of 77 (10%). 67 posts link to a PDF, 7 to Hugging Face and 1 to Sketchfab ([Posts API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/posts)). Meanwhile the `iaac-maai` GitHub organisation holds **123 public repositories**. Of the latest 100, none has GitHub topics and only 3 have a homepage link, and only 3 blog posts link to the organisation ([GitHub org](https://api.github.com/orgs/iaac-maai); [repos](https://api.github.com/orgs/iaac-maai/repos?per_page=100&sort=created)). Repository names also vary, for example "Hackathon_Group_08", "Hackaton_Group3_AIMethods" and "group-3-day4-assignment". The code and the writing about the code are in two systems that do not point to each other.

The measured quality problems are concrete. **163 titles occur more than once (402 posts)**, and 329 post URLs end in "-2", "-3" and so on. 23 posts have numeric-only URLs and 7 have no title ([Posts API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/posts)). **521 posts (12%) have fewer than 30 words of text**. 320 posts embed images pasted from Google Docs (hosted on googleusercontent.com), and in a sample of 40 of these images, 3 were broken. Example posts are [Bund Finance Centre](https://blog.iaac.net/bund-finance-centre/) and [Introduction to digital fabrication](https://blog.iaac.net/introduction-to-digital-fabrication/). The sample is small, so present it as "about 1 in 13 in a sample", not as a blog-wide rate. Images uploaded to the blog itself loaded correctly (60 of 60). The programme pages show an unrendered code fragment, `[print-menu name="programmes"]`, as visible text, and the Syllabi year filter has no 2026-2027 option ([Syllabi](https://blog.iaac.net/syllabi/)). Finally, older work from 2011 to 2022 is on a separate site, iaacblog.com, so one search cannot cover both archives ([Home page footer](https://blog.iaac.net/); [iaacblog.com](https://www.iaacblog.com/)). That site refused automated access, so check it in a browser before you describe what it contains.

One technical note matters if the atlas reuses blog data. The raw REST API content still contains `blog.iaac.net/wp-content/...` image URLs, and these return 404. The public HTML rewrites them to `blog-media.iaac.net`. The atlas must apply the same rewrite ([Media API](https://blog.iaac.net/wp-json/wp/v2/media?per_page=1)).

## A slime mould, a railway and the skeleton of the universe

The method story is a chain of four verified steps, and each step changes the domain while it keeps the same rule: grow a network between points, then strengthen the routes that carry the most flow.

**The cosmic web.** Matter in the universe is not spread evenly. Gravity pulls it into clusters, filaments, walls and voids. In simulation, **filaments hold about 50% of the mass in about 6% of the volume**, and **voids fill 77% of the volume but hold only 15% of the mass** ([Cautun et al. 2014, arXiv:1401.7866](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1401.7866)). The Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics also reports that filaments contain over half of the universe's matter ([MPA, 2024](https://www.mpa-garching.mpg.de/1098309/hl202406)). Luminous galaxies hold less than 10% of normal matter; most of the rest is diffuse gas that scientists expected along the filaments but could not easily see ([Burchett et al. 2020](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2003.04393)).

**The Tokyo experiment.** Tero and colleagues put **36 oat-flake food sources** in the positions of cities around Tokyo and let the slime mould *Physarum polycephalum* grow from "Tokyo". It first filled the space and then thinned to a few thick tubes. Snapshots run from 0 to 26 hours. The team lit the sea and mountain areas, because the mould avoids light, and this made the network more like the real rail map ([Tero et al. 2010, Science 327:439](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/files/papers/others/2010/tero2010a.pdf)). The numbers are strong enough for one slide. Measured against the shortest possible network, the rail system costs about 1.8 times as much and the mould 1.75 ± 0.30. Transport efficiency was **0.85 for both**. The rail network was more robust: only 4% of single faults cut it, against 14 ± 4% for the lit mould. The authors say the mould did this "without centralized control or explicit global information" ([Tero et al. 2010](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/files/papers/others/2010/tero2010a.pdf); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20093467/)). For an architecture jury, note that cost, efficiency and fault tolerance are the same trade-offs used in infrastructure planning.

**The Jones agent model.** Jeff Jones replaced the mould with a population of simple particle agents. Each agent senses a trail map with three sensors, turns toward the strongest signal, moves one step and deposits trail; the trail then diffuses and decays. A transport network appears with no central plan ([Jones 2010, Artificial Life 16(2)](https://uwe-repository.worktribe.com/output/980579); [Sage Jenson's explainer](https://cargocollective.com/sagejenson/physarum)). Six parameters control the result: sensor distance, sensor angle, rotation angle, step size, deposit and decay. Check the page range of the Jones paper at its DOI (10.1162/artl.2010.16.2.16202) before you print it.

**MCPM and the cosmic web.** Elek, Burchett and colleagues made a probabilistic, 3D version of the Jones model, the Monte Carlo Physarum Machine ([Elek et al. 2022, Artificial Life 28(1)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01256)). Each galaxy became a fixed food source that emits trail in proportion to its mass, and a separate "trace" field recorded where agents travelled. That trace became the density map ([Polyphorm, IEEE TVCG 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.02441)). They first tested it on about 840,000 simulated dark-matter haloes and then ran it on **about 37,000 real SDSS galaxies with 10 million agents**. It converged in about 75 seconds on one GPU ([Polyphorm](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.02441); [NASA, 10 Mar 2020](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/slime-mold-simulations-used-to-map-dark-matter-holding-universe-together/)). The team then checked light from **about 350 background quasars** in the Hubble archive and found hydrogen gas along the predicted filaments, even far from galaxies. Their conclusion: "the bulk of the IGM indeed resides in the Cosmic Web" ([Burchett et al. 2020, ApJL 891:L35](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2003.04393)). Burchett's line works well in a script: "one of the simplest forms of life actually enables insight into the very largest-scale structures in the universe" ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/slime-mold-simulations-used-to-map-dark-matter-holding-universe-together/)).

**From galaxies to ideas.** The step to a map of research has a direct precedent. Zhou, Elek and colleagues ran MCPM on word embeddings, which are points in an abstract meaning space, not in physical space. They found one densely connected network, and the work became the Polyglot tool in the PolyPhy toolkit ([arXiv 2009.02459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.02459); [Polyglot](https://normand-1024.github.io/Bio-inspired-Exploration-of-Language-Embedding)). Science mapping is also an established field. Börner's *Atlas of Science* (MIT Press, 2010) collects more than 30 science maps ([History of Information](https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?entryid=3789)), and Rosvall and Bergstrom mapped 6,128 journals and 6.4 million citations into 88 modules ([arXiv 0707.0609](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.0609)). Be precise about novelty: the research found no earlier use of a Physarum algorithm to map a body of research projects, and no source that uses "cosmic web" as a metaphor for a science map. MaAI Atlas can claim this combination as its own step, built on cited parts.

The analogy for the atlas is the presenter's own mapping, not a claim from the sources. Say so on the slide.

| Cosmic web (MCPM) | MaAI Atlas |
|---|---|
| Galaxy (fixed food source, weighted by mass) | Project (blog post plus repo) |
| Filament (high trace density) | Shared method, tool or theme |
| Cluster node | Research hub where many projects meet |
| Void | Topic no project has explored yet |
| Quasar test of predicted gas | Check predicted links against real reuse or citations |

The last row is worth keeping. The astronomers did not stop at a beautiful picture; they tested it with independent data. The atlas becomes more convincing if it names its own test.

## The Sagrada Família in November 2026: skyline done, front door open

The Sagrada Família is a fitting metaphor because it is a collective project that passed between generations, lost its source material, and had to rebuild a method from fragments. It is also a Barcelona building, which the IAAC jury sees every day.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 19 Mar 1882 | First stone laid, with a neo-Gothic design by Francisco de Paula del Villar |
| 1883 | Gaudí takes over and redesigns the project |
| 10 Jun 1926 | Gaudí dies; his disciple Domènec Sugranyes continues |
| 1936 | Workshop burned; plans and photographs lost; plaster models smashed |
| 7 Nov 2010 | Consecrated as a basilica by Benedict XVI |
| 12 Nov 2023 | All four Evangelist towers (135 m) inaugurated |
| 20 Feb 2026 | Upper arm of the cross installed; Tower of Jesus Christ exterior complete at 172.5 m |
| 10 Jun 2026 | Gaudí centenary; Pope Leo XIV blesses the tower, which is lit for the first time |
| about 2035 to 2036 | Forecast end of works |

Sources: [sagradafamilia.org history](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/history-of-the-temple?lang=0); [Vatican News, Feb 2026](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/church/news/2026-02/basilica-sagrada-familia-cross-completed-tower-of-jesus-christ.html); [sagradafamilia.org blessing page](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/detalle/-/asset_publisher/7JWibz9G2KUY/content/benediccio-i-inauguracio-de-la-torre-de-jesucrist?_com_liferay_asset_publisher_web_portlet_AssetPublisherPortlet_INSTANCE_7JWibz9G2KUY_assetEntryId=1721008); [Catalan News, 6 Jun 2026](https://www.catalannews.com/popes-visit/item/sagrada-familia-timeline-of-a-temple-under-construction-for-144-years); [Ara, 6 Jun 2026](https://en.ara.cat/culture/these-will-be-the-great-milestones-of-the-sagrada-familia-until-2035_1_5760542.html).

Three parallels carry the metaphor. First, **lost sources and a rebuilt method**. After 1936, later architects continued from saved material and from models rebuilt from published plans and photographs ([sagradafamilia.org](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/history-of-the-temple?lang=0)). Mark Burry used parametric software from the aerospace industry to recover Gaudí's geometry from model fragments ([Communications of the ACM](https://cacm.acm.org/news/a-model-restoration); [Burry, arq](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/arq-architectural-research-quarterly/article/abs/parametric-design-and-the-sagrada-familia/5D63CFAC6A348CF4D6148D93EDC4CDB3)). Since 2001 the team has printed plaster models in 3D, and CNC machines cut stone from the digital model ([3D Systems case study](https://au.3dsystems.com/learning-center/case-studies/sagrada-familia-color-jet-printing-cjp-helps-architects-sagrada-familia); [Arup](https://www.arup.com/sagrada-familia)). This is the exact situation of a student who must continue work from a broken link, a missing image or an unlinked repository. Second, **many hands across generations**. The official history names **at least nine head architects since 1882**, from Villar to Jordi Faulí, who leads a team of about 20 architects today ([sagradafamilia.org history](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/history-of-the-temple?lang=0); [booklet](https://sagradafamilia.org/documents/20142/1000561/Booklet_03.pdf/7cdf5437-1385-3ac7-76d8-f391ca884e34)). Third, **many small contributions**. The temple takes no public money. About 97% of its income comes from visitor tickets: €134.5 million in 2025 from 4,877,567 visitors ([sagradafamilia.org annual report 2025](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/report); [Ara, 5 Jun 2026](https://en.ara.cat/economy/the-sagrada-familia-s-finances-135m-turnover-and-1-600-jobs_1_5759214.html)). Each cohort's projects are the research programme's equivalent.

The strongest image for November 2026 is the unbuilt entrance. The skyline is complete, but most of the remaining work is at the Glory façade, the main entrance. Its four apostle towers are still in design, and its sculpture programme is undecided ([Ara, 6 Jun 2026](https://en.ara.cat/culture/these-will-be-the-great-milestones-of-the-sagrada-familia-until-2035_1_5760542.html)). Gaudí's staircase would cross Carrer de Mallorca. On 22 September 2026 the temple corrected its own director and said the project "will entail impacts on other buildings in the vicinity", and no City Council decision has followed ([Ara, 22 Sep 2026](https://en.ara.cat/society/the-sagrada-familia-rectifies-and-specifies-that-the-staircase-will-have-to-affect-more-than-one-building_1_5857199.html)). The parallel for the talk is direct: MaAI has strong projects, but the public "front door" to them is missing. The atlas proposes that door. The deadline story also helps: the old target of completion for the 2026 centenary slipped, partly because of the COVID-19 pause ([Catalan News](https://www.catalannews.com/popes-visit/item/sagrada-familia-timeline-of-a-temple-under-construction-for-144-years)).

Do not overstate three details. The quote "My client is not in a hurry" has **no primary source**; write "attributed to Gaudí" ([Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/has-gaudis-sagrada-familia-has-grown-out-of-touch.md)). The completion date is a forecast, not official: Ara says around 2035 and Catalan News says 2036. "14 of 18 towers complete" is a count derived from cited sources, not a stated figure, so check it on the official site before you put it on a slide.

## One claim per slide, the rest in your voice

The assertion-evidence method replaces a topic heading and bullets with a **full-sentence claim as the heading and a visual as the evidence** ([assertion-evidence.com](https://www.assertion-evidence.com/)). In a controlled study, the audience that saw assertion-evidence slides understood and remembered the content better (p < .01) ([Garner et al. 2013, IJEE 29(6)](https://www.ijee.ie/articles/Vol29-6/23_ijee2791ns.pdf); [Penn State research page](https://writing.engr.psu.edu/research.html)). An earlier study found the advantage was largest for complex concepts and one week later ([ASEE 2011](https://peer.asee.org/assertion-evidence-slides-appear-to-lead-to-better-comprehension-and-recall-of-more-complex-concepts.pdf)). Be honest about the limit: a 2012 follow-up found only a non-significant trend, and the topic-heading group improved when it had good visuals. Visual quality carries much of the benefit ([Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/high-quality-visual-evidence-on-presentation-slides-may-offset-th-2/)). Most of these studies come from one research group. Students who built assertion-evidence slides also understood their own content better, which is a useful side effect before a jury ([ASEE PEER](https://peer.asee.org/how-slide-design-affects-a-student-presenter-s-understanding-of-the-content)).

Mayer's multimedia principles give the design rules. **Coherence**: remove decoration (supported in 18 of 19 tests, median effect size 0.86). **Signalling**: highlight the essential part (26 of 28 tests, effect size 0.70). **Redundancy**: do not print what you say (smaller effect, 0.10) ([Mayer, UNH PDF](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf)). **Segmenting**: show a complex diagram in steps ([Dartmouth](https://services.dartmouth.edu/TDClient/1806/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=171655)). Applied to MaAI Atlas, the MCPM explanation becomes three builds: points, agents, trace.

For the script, plan **130 to 150 spoken words per minute** ([Plus AI](https://plusai.com/blog/words-per-minute-for-speeches-and-presentations)); Microsoft's Speaker Coach accepts 100 to 165 ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/PowerPoint/suggestions-from-speaker-coach)). A 10-minute talk is then about 1,300 to 1,500 words. Give complex visuals fewer words so the audience has time to look. Archinect advises memorising the main points, not the full text, and rehearsing aloud with a recording ([Archinect](https://www.archinect.com/features/article/150145350/the-architecture-student-s-presentation-roadmap)). A practical per-slide pattern follows from the method (an inference, not a tested rule): say the claim, point at the evidence, give one consequence, then ask the question the next slide answers.

"Generic" slides have recognisable signs. Practitioner sources list purple-to-blue gradients, gradient headline text, glass cards, glows, three equal cards in a row, a centred hero, one default font everywhere, and an icon or emoji on every item ([Anti AI slop ban list](https://github.com/Laith0003/ux-skill/wiki/Anti-AI-slop-ban-list); [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/ai-design-slop-era)). Wikipedia's guide adds writing signs: "not X but Y" constructions, automatic lists of three, puffery and heavy use of em dashes ([Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). These are design opinions, not measured research, but they match Mayer's coherence rule. The best defence for this talk is your own evidence: the real blog counts, the NASA cosmic-web image ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/slime-mold-simulations-used-to-map-dark-matter-holding-universe-together/)), a real photo of the Glory façade site, and the atlas itself. Architecture juries ask for the same thing: few words, large drawings readable from 2 to 3 metres, and fewer drawings explained well ([learnarchitecture.net](https://learnarchitecture.net/articles/34015-architecture-presentation-tips.html); [Archinect](https://www.archinect.com/features/article/150145350/the-architecture-student-s-presentation-roadmap)). Check image licences before use: the NASA slime-mould image is co-credited to Burchett and Elek, and the Tero figures belong to AAAS/Science.

## Conclusion

The four parts of the talk share one argument. The blog problem is a missing-structure problem, not a missing-content problem: thousands of posts exist, but the links between them are invisible. That is the same situation the astronomers faced with diffuse gas, and they solved it by growing a network between known points and then testing it with independent data. The Sagrada Família adds time to this argument. A collective work survives the loss of its sources only if later contributors can recover the method, and in November 2026 its unfinished part is precisely the public entrance.

The most useful next step for credibility is to borrow the astronomers' validation habit. Define one independent check for the atlas, for example whether linked projects share repositories, tools or tutors, and show it on a slide. Also treat the measured blog problems as a design brief for the atlas: a stable project ID that joins post, repository and video; method tags; and images stored on the blog's own media server. Three facts still need a manual check before the final deck: the iaacblog.com archive in a browser, the "14 of 18 towers" count, and the Jones 2010 page range.
