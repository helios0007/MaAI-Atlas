# Sagrada Família as a metaphor for a collective, unfinished research programme

Status date of these notes: 25 November 2026. Every claim has a source and, where possible, the date of the source.

## 1. Key dates and timeline (1882 to November 2026)

### Takeaway
The building started in 1882 with another architect's design. Gaudí took over in 1883 and worked on it for 43 years. After his death (1926) and the destruction of his workshop (1936), later architects rebuilt his models from fragments and continued the work. The highest tower (Jesus Christ, 172.5 m) got its exterior finished on 20 Feb 2026 and was blessed on 10 June 2026 (Gaudí centenary). The Glory façade is not built. The current forecast for the end of the works is about 2035 to 2036.

### Inferences
- For the metaphor: as of Nov 2026 the "skyline" is complete (all six central towers), but the main entrance, which gives access to the whole building, is not built and depends on negotiation with neighbours. This is a good image for a research programme: the core results exist, but the "front door" for the public is still open work.
- The original 2026 completion target slipped to about 2035 to 2036. Deadlines in long collective projects move.

### Gaps
- Exact final completion date is not official. Sources give "around 2035" (Ara) and 2036 (Catalan News). Decorative elements could go later.
- Public opening date of the Tower of Jesus Christ interior / viewpoint: not confirmed. Secondary sites say 2027; not verified.
- Old figure "3,000 homes / 1,000 families affected" by the staircase appears in older press (Designboom); I could not confirm it for 2026. Do not use without a check.
- No new decision on the staircase found between 22 Sep and 25 Nov 2026.

## 2. Funding, number of architects/generations, digital tools

### Takeaway
It is an "expiatory temple": no public money, it is paid by private donations. In the 20th century donations and inheritances paid for it; today about 97% of income comes from visitor tickets (4.88 million visitors in 2025, €134.5 M income). At least 9 head architects/site directors have led it since 1882 (Villar, Gaudí, Sugranyes, Quintana, Puig i Boada and Bonet i Garí, Cardoner, Jordi Bonet, Faulí). From about 1979 to 1990s, Mark Burry and others used aerospace/parametric software to decode Gaudí's geometry from model fragments; from 2001 they used 3D printing; stone is cut by CNC from digital files.

### Cited Findings
Funding:
- Legally a canonical foundation financed by donations; in the 20th century donations and inheritances were most of the income; 2025 income €134.5 M, all private; 51.7% to construction, 31.8% to temple management; 4,877,567 visitors in 2025 (+0.91% on 2024); no inheritances in 2025 — [sagradafamilia.org annual report 2025](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/report); [sagradafamilia.org news](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/-/la-sagrada-familia-consolida-la-seva-activitat-economica-i-estrena-nova-identitat-visual)
- Practically 97% of income comes from visitors; expenses €113.5 M; construction almost €59 M; the temple invests €50 to 70 M a year; about 1,600 people work on the temple and its two stone workshops, about 150 of them temple staff — [Ara, 5 Jun 2026](https://en.ara.cat/economy/the-sagrada-familia-s-finances-135m-turnover-and-1-600-jobs_1_5759214.html)
- Mid-20th-century public collections paid for the early works; tourism income later sped up construction; the pandemic cut income — [Catalan News, 6 Jun 2026](https://www.catalannews.com/popes-visit/item/sagrada-familia-timeline-of-a-temple-under-construction-for-144-years)

Architects / generations (official succession):
- 1882 Villar; 1883 Gaudí; 1926 Domènec Sugranyes; 1939 Francesc de Paula Quintana; 1966 Isidre Puig i Boada and Lluís Bonet i Garí; 1983 Francesc Cardoner i Blanch; 1985 Jordi Bonet i Armengol; 2012 Jordi Faulí — [sagradafamilia.org history](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/history-of-the-temple?lang=0)
- Faulí leads a team of about 20 architects (coordinated by David Puig) — [sagradafamilia.org booklet](https://sagradafamilia.org/documents/20142/1000561/Booklet_03.pdf/7cdf5437-1385-3ac7-76d8-f391ca884e34)
- Aleteia (24 Mar 2026) on "the many hands behind Sagrada Familia" — [Aleteia](https://www.aleteia.org/2026/03/24/the-many-hands-behind-sagrada-familia/) (not fetched)

Digital tools:
- After the 1936 destruction, Mark Burry used parametric tools from the aeronautical industry to reconstruct Gaudí's geometry; first looked at boat-hull software but licences (Intergraph, about US$75,000) were too expensive; team used Computervision software, which moved to parametric modelling — [Communications of the ACM, "A model restoration"](https://cacm.acm.org/news/a-model-restoration)
- Academic paper: Burry, "Parametric design and the Sagrada Família", arq (Architectural Research Quarterly), Cambridge University Press — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/arq-architectural-research-quarterly/article/abs/parametric-design-and-the-sagrada-familia/5D63CFAC6A348CF4D6148D93EDC4CDB3)
- Burry later helped Frank Gehry's office develop parametric software; the project pioneered 3D printing for model-making and lidar scanning — [99% Invisible](https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/la-sagrada-familia/); [Mark Burry, Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Burry)
- Since 2001 architects Jordi Faulí, Jordi Coll and Mark Burry used 3D printers to make plaster models (about 12 h per model); technical office bought 3D Systems Spectrum Z510 printers — [3D Systems case study](https://au.3dsystems.com/learning-center/case-studies/sagrada-familia-color-jet-printing-cjp-helps-architects-sagrada-familia); [NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/sagrada-familia-3d-printing/2000793/)
- Stone pieces cut by fast CNC cutters from digital patterns; digital model drives automatic cutting of stone blocks for prefabricated panels — [Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/technology/technology-gives-sagrada-familia-an-end-date_o.aspx); [Arup](https://www.arup.com/sagrada-familia)
- Virtual files go to stone workshops (e.g. Granits Barbany) to be made in stone — [Sagrada Família blog](https://blog.sagradafamilia.org/en/?p=4504)
- Materialise software used for data preparation of 3D-print files — [Materialise case study](https://www.materialise.com/en/cases/sagrada-familia-magics-meets-modernism)

### Inferences
- About 6 generations of builders over 144 years (1882 to 2026): Gaudí's own team, his disciples (Sugranyes, Quintana), the post-war team (Puig i Boada, Bonet i Garí), the Bonet/Cardoner team, and the Faulí digital-era team. This is my count, not an official number.
- Strong metaphor point: Gaudí left a design method (ruled geometric surfaces: hyperboloids, paraboloids, helicoids) and broken models, not a complete drawing set. Later generations had to rebuild the method from fragments and new tools (parametric software) to continue. Students who extend a shared research map do the same.
- Funding by many small "donations" (tickets) from millions of visitors is a second parallel: many small contributions, not one sponsor.

### Gaps
- Exact year Mark Burry started (often given as 1979) not confirmed from a primary source in this research.
- Total cumulative construction cost: only a secondary figure (about €400 M) found; not reliable.
- Many 3D-printing/CNC details come from 2015 to 2017 vendor case studies; current equipment not verified.
- Some secondary sources give different dates for the post-Gaudí succession (e.g. Sugranyes died 1938; Quintana in 1939 vs 1952). The official history page is used above.

## 3. Gaudí's "my client is not in a hurry" quote

### Takeaway
The quote is famous but no primary source was found. Common English wording: "My client is not in a hurry." Spanish: "Mi cliente no tiene prisa." The "client" is God. It is usually dated to the early 1920s (some say a 1922 architects' congress in Barcelona). Present it as "attributed to Gaudí".

### Cited Findings
- Wording varies: "My client is not in a hurry", "My client isn't in a hurry", "My client can wait"; usually introduced with "reportedly" or "is said to" — [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/has-gaudis-sagrada-familia-has-grown-out-of-touch.md); [UnHerd, Jun 2026](https://unherd.com/2026/06/the-indignity-of-gaudi-lego/?set_edition=en)
- Spanish version "Mi cliente no tiene prisa", with the joke that the client is God — [barcelona-tickets.com (commercial)](https://www.barcelona-tickets.com/es/sagrada-familia-tickets/antoni-gaudi/)
- Catalan line "Déu no té pressa" appears in Carlos Ruiz Zafón's fiction "Gaudí en Manhattan" (fiction, not a record) — [XTEC PDF](https://agora.xtec.cat/prova2/wp-content/uploads/usu151/2025/05/gaudi_manhattan.pdf)

### Inferences
- Safe slide wording: "My client is not in a hurry." — attributed to Antoni Gaudí (1920s), referring to God.

### Gaps
- No contemporary document (letter, newspaper, or biography with citation, e.g. by Joan Bassegoda Nonell) found. The 1922 congress context is from tourist guides only. FLAG: wording and date unverified.

## 4. Main parts of the building

### Takeaway
Latin-cross plan with five naves; three façades (Nativity – east, built mostly in Gaudí's lifetime; Passion – west; Glory – south main entrance, not built); 18 towers planned (12 apostles, 4 evangelists, Virgin Mary, Jesus Christ); nave with tree-like branching columns that make a "forest".

### Cited Findings
- 18 towers: 12 apostles (98.5 to 120 m, four on each façade), 4 evangelists (135 m), Virgin Mary (138 m), Jesus Christ (172.5 m, tallest) — [sagradafamilia.org booklet](https://sagradafamilia.org/documents/20142/1000561/Booklet_04.pdf/5eb8514a-7bd2-77d0-15dc-9c9c0315cf23); heights also in [sagradafamilia.org history](https://sagradafamilia.org/en/history-of-the-temple?lang=0); [Vatican News](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/church/news/2026-02/basilica-sagrada-familia-cross-completed-tower-of-jesus-christ.html)
- Three façades: Nativity, Passion, Glory (future); Latin-cross plan; tree-inspired columns branching from a trunk, support five naves (forest effect) — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/764813); [La Pedrera Foundation](https://www.lapedrera.com/en/work-antoni-gaudi/sagrada-familia/)
- Nativity façade and crypt are a UNESCO World Heritage site (2005) — [Catalan News](https://www.catalannews.com/popes-visit/item/sagrada-familia-timeline-of-a-temple-under-construction-for-144-years)
- Main nave, transepts and apse: foundations from the mid-1980s, finished as a whole by 2010 — [Catalan News](https://www.catalannews.com/popes-visit/item/sagrada-familia-timeline-of-a-temple-under-construction-for-144-years)
- Still to build (2026): Glory façade with 4 apostle towers, portico and staircase; chapels of Penance and of the Sacrament and baptistery; north sacristy; Chapel of the Assumption (mid-2027); roof lanterns and pinnacle — [Ara, 6 Jun 2026](https://en.ara.cat/culture/these-will-be-the-great-milestones-of-the-sagrada-familia-until-2035_1_5760542.html)

### Inferences
- Tower count as of Nov 2026 (my count from cited sources): 8 apostle towers (Nativity + Passion façades) + 4 evangelists + Mary + Jesus = 14 of 18 complete on the exterior; the 4 Glory-façade apostle towers remain. Secondary sources disagree on counts from older dates; check with the official site before a slide says "14 of 18".

### Gaps
- Number of columns (36 is often given) is from secondary sources only; not verified on the official site.
- The exact attribution of each façade's completion date (Passion façade sculptures by Subirachs 1987 onward) not researched here.
